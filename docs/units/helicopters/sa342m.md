## Background

The Aérospatiale SA 342M Gazelle is the French Army (ALAT) variant specifically developed as a light anti-tank helicopter. The M-model entered service in 1980 armed with the Euromissile HOT (Haut subsonique Optiquement Téléguidé) wire-guided anti-tank missile system, with four missiles carried on outrigger pylons. The crew of two—pilot and gunner—engage targets using a roof-mounted APX M397 gyro-stabilized sight that provides magnified optics for target acquisition and missile guidance.

The SA 342M saw combat during French operations in Chad during the 1980s, where Gazelles armed with HOT missiles engaged Libyan armor during the "Toyota War" and subsequent interventions. French Army Gazelles also deployed to Lebanon, the Gulf War, Kosovo, and numerous African peacekeeping operations. The combination of the Gazelle's agility and the HOT missile's precision made it effective against armored vehicles in environments ranging from desert to mountain terrain.

The HOT system's wire-guidance requires the helicopter to maintain line-of-sight throughout the missile's flight, typically 15-20 seconds to maximum range—a vulnerability that later systems with fire-and-forget capability addressed. However, the relatively low cost of the Gazelle compared to dedicated attack helicopters like the Apache made it an economical solution for nations requiring anti-tank helicopter capability. The SA 342M served as the French Army's primary anti-tank helicopter until replaced by the Eurocopter Tiger, though Gazelles remained in service for training and secondary roles. In DCS, the SA 342M is flyable as part of the Gazelle module.
