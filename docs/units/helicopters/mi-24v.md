## Background

The Mil Mi-24V (NATO reporting name "Hind-E") is the most widely produced variant of the Mi-24 family, entering service with Soviet forces in 1976. The V-model introduced the improved USPU-24 chin turret mounting the four-barrel 12.7mm YakB-12.7 rotary machine gun, capable of 4,000 rounds per minute. More significantly, the Mi-24V was the first variant to carry the 9M114 Shturm (NATO: AT-6 Spiral) radio-command guided anti-tank missile, providing standoff capability against armored vehicles at ranges up to 5 kilometers.

The Mi-24V bore the brunt of Soviet helicopter operations in Afghanistan. Hinds flew thousands of sorties supporting ground forces, striking mujahideen caravans, providing armed escort for Mi-8 transports, and inserting spetsnaz teams into mountain positions. Soviet pilots developed innovative tactics to deal with the threat from MANPADS, including high-speed attack runs and coordinated flights with electronic warfare aircraft. The chaff and flare dispensers fitted to Mi-24Vs became essential for survival as Stingers proliferated among Afghan resistance fighters.

The Mi-24V has served with Soviet successor states and export customers worldwide. Iraqi Hinds saw extensive combat during the Iran-Iraq War, engaging Iranian ground forces and even achieving air-to-air kills against Iranian helicopters. Syrian Mi-24Vs have operated throughout the civil war that began in 2011, conducting strikes against opposition forces. The type remains in service with numerous nations and has proven remarkably adaptable to different combat environments and threat conditions.
