## Background

The SA 342 Mistral is a Gazelle variant armed with Mistral infrared-homing air-to-air missiles for helicopter self-defense and light air defense duties. The Mistral (Missile Transportable Léger Anti-aérien) is a French-developed short-range air-to-air missile originally designed as a man-portable surface-to-air missile, adapted for helicopter use with the ATAM (Air-to-Air Mistral) launcher system. The missile homes on infrared emissions from target aircraft or helicopters, achieving kills at ranges up to 6 kilometers.

The Mistral-armed Gazelle emerged from recognition that light helicopters operating in contested environments required some means of self-defense against enemy rotorcraft. While not intended to compete with dedicated attack helicopters in the air-to-air role, the Mistral provides a credible deterrent against opposing scout and transport helicopters. French Army Gazelles in the Celtic system paired Mistral-armed helicopters with HOT-armed variants, with the former providing top cover against air threats while the latter engaged ground targets.

This configuration has limited ground attack capability, relying primarily on the Mistral missiles for engaging aerial targets. The role is highly specialized—providing helicopter air defense and escort for vulnerable transport formations. In environments where enemy helicopters pose a threat, the Mistral Gazelle provides a cost-effective counter. In DCS, the SA 342 Mistral is flyable as part of the Gazelle module.
