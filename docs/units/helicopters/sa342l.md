## Background

The Aérospatiale SA 342L Gazelle is a light scout and utility helicopter of French origin. First flying in 1967 and entering service in 1973, the Gazelle was developed as a successor to the Alouette series and introduced several innovative features including the distinctive fenestron shrouded tail rotor—a ducted fan design that reduces noise, improves safety around ground personnel, and provides better tail rotor efficiency in crosswind conditions. The L variant is an export model featuring the Astazou XIV engine with improved hot-and-high performance and compatibility with a variety of armament options.

The Gazelle has seen extensive combat service with numerous operators. British Army Gazelles deployed to the Falklands War in 1982, providing reconnaissance and light transport despite losses to Argentine ground fire. French Gazelles armed with HOT missiles served in Chad during operations against Libyan forces in the 1980s. Syrian Gazelles engaged Israeli forces during the 1982 Lebanon War. Iraqi Gazelles saw action during the Iran-Iraq War and the Gulf War, with some captured by coalition forces. Yugoslav Gazelles operated during the Balkan conflicts of the 1990s.

The SA 342L's light weight and agility make it well-suited for reconnaissance, observation, and light attack missions in environments where larger attack helicopters would be too conspicuous or expensive to operate. The helicopter can be armed with rockets, machine gun pods, or Mistral air-to-air missiles for self-defense and light air defense duties. Over 1,200 Gazelles were produced, and the type remains in service with armed forces in Africa, Asia, and the Middle East.
