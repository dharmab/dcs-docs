## Background

The Bell OH-58D Kiowa Warrior is an armed reconnaissance helicopter developed from the earlier OH-58A/C Kiowa scout helicopter under the Army Helicopter Improvement Program (AHIP). Entering service in 1985, the OH-58D introduced the distinctive Mast Mounted Sight (MMS)—a spherical sensor turret mounted above the main rotor containing television, thermal imaging, and laser designator/rangefinder systems. This configuration allows the Kiowa to observe and designate targets while remaining concealed behind terrain with only the sensor ball exposed, a tactic pioneered in response to the increasing lethality of anti-aircraft systems.

The OH-58D first saw combat during Operation Prime Chance in 1987-1988, when specially modified Kiowa Warriors conducted night attacks against Iranian vessels laying mines in the Persian Gulf during the Tanker War. Armed with Hellfire missiles and .50 caliber machine guns, OH-58Ds destroyed or damaged numerous Iranian speedboats and small craft. The type subsequently deployed to Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia, Haiti, Bosnia, Kosovo, Afghanistan, and Iraq, demonstrating remarkable versatility across two decades of operations.

In Iraq and Afghanistan, Kiowa Warrior teams became essential for reconnaissance and close air support of ground forces. The helicopter's small size, agility, and relatively low operating costs made it ideal for sustained presence over the battlefield. OH-58D crews developed close relationships with ground units, providing overwatch and engaging targets with rockets, Hellfires, and machine guns. The type was officially retired from U.S. Army service in 2017, replaced by a combination of AH-64E Apaches and unmanned systems, though it remains in service with some allied nations.
