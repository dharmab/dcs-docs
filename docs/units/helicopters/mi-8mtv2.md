## Background

The Mil Mi-8MTV2 (NATO reporting name "Hip-H") is an upgraded variant of the venerable Mi-8 transport helicopter, featuring more powerful TV3-117VM turboshaft engines and improved avionics for all-weather operations. The Mi-8 family has been produced in greater numbers than any other helicopter in history—over 17,000 built since 1961—and serves in military and civilian roles in more than 80 countries worldwide.

The Mi-8 earned its reputation in Afghanistan, where Soviet Hip transports flew countless missions delivering troops, supplies, and casualties through hostile mountain valleys. The helicopter proved rugged and maintainable in austere conditions, surviving damage that would have downed lesser aircraft. Armed versions carried rocket pods and machine guns for suppressive fire during insertions into hot landing zones. The Mi-8's ability to operate from high-altitude forward operating bases made it indispensable for the Soviet campaign.

The MTV2 variant, entering service in the 1990s, incorporated lessons from Afghanistan and Chechnya. The upgraded engines provide additional power margins for hot-and-high operations, while improved avionics include weather radar, GPS navigation, and enhanced communication systems. The helicopter can carry up to 24 troops or 4 tons of internal cargo, and retains capability for armament including rocket pods, bombs, and door-mounted machine guns.

Russian Mi-8MTVs have deployed to Syria and Ukraine, providing transport and fire support. The type serves with former Soviet republics, China, India, and numerous other nations in both military and civilian configurations. In DCS, the Mi-8MTV2 is a high-fidelity module offering authentic Hip flight dynamics and systems simulation.
